Defamiliarization and Dissociation of Sensibility are techniques from perception-based methods. These methods shift reality to how it is perceived, and they are reader-centric. They emphasize estrangement and fragmentation. Defamiliarization makes usual things fresh in a strange way, and Dissociation of Sensibility splits what you feel in your body from what you think in your head. They enhance perceptual disruption when combined.

## Defamiliarization

Defamiliarization is a button for readers. It makes you stop and see familiar things freshly, like the first time. It doesn’t show them as new but it makes you notice the unfamiliarity from the things that you are familiar with. This technique helps readers see details in their mundane life. Automatization becomes strange.

## Dissociation of Sensibility

Dissociation of Sensibility splits body feelings from what you think in your head. The mind and the body feelings work separately or delay. When you want your work to be done but the bed feels good, here they don’t follow a decision. Readers connect it with the morning paralysis. Therefore, your feelings are not fused together.

Notes: Defamiliarization hits the outside world and Dissociation of Sensibility splits inside you—different perception layers.

## How They Work Together

- Defamiliarization and Dissociation of Sensibility make double perceptual impact.
- Defamiliarization uses odd words or angles to slow down and notice surface grit. A cockroach crawls the floor—it doesn’t just crawl but violates floor space. You think about house management and realize you haven’t cleaned properly. You doubt your capabilities. Everything you own feels strange now.
- Dissociation of Sensibility then splits the inner response. You feel disgusted by the unclean house horror, but goals stay your priority. You keep routine like autopilot, but feelings don’t fade—they stay disconnected. This shows Dissociation of Sensibility perfectly.

## How to Use in Poetry for Beginner

Defamiliarization and dissociation help beginners make readers feel, not just read.

### Defamiliarization (Make world strange)

- Shoes → “clamps my feet like trap”
- Pen → “bleeds blue secrets”
- Normal → weird action verbs

### Dissociation (Split body vs mind)

- Body sense → separate → head thought
- No connection between lines
- I got to get to work/bed feels cozy

### Combine

My shoes are waiting for me<br>
To clamp my feet like trap<br>
I got to get to work<br>
The bed feels cozy

## 5-Min Practice

Object near you → weird verb + body feel + daily thought

Desk grips wrists. Sweat beads. Deadline blinks.

## Conclusion

Defamiliarization and Dissociation of Sensibility are a strong duo that changes a flat description into raw perceptual experience. Defamiliarization opens the familiar world, shatters the automatization so readers notice specific details that are usually skipped. Dissociation of sensibility then splits the inner self into body feelings and head thoughts. It causes a familiar paralysis where senses and logic refuse to unite. Together they trap readers in human messiness. The outside reality becomes alien while inside responses fragments, making every poem pulse recognize rather than just understand it. Beginners can start small—short, rhyme-free lines that hit hard and linger forever.
